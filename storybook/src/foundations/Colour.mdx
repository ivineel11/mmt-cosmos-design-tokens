# Colour

Semantic colours are grouped by **role** (background, surface, fill, text, border, icon), not by hue. Within a role, the suffix carries the **intent** (`brand`, `info`, `success`, `caution`, `warning`), the intensity (`strong`, `subtle`) and the interaction step (`hover`, `pressed`). In Cosmos, `warning` is the red error role and `caution` is amber.

Use semantic tokens in product code. Primitives exist so a palette can be re-tinted in one place.

## Canvas and container pairing

A screen sits on one of two canvases, white or grey, and the container on top must be the other colour. Match the suffix: `bg` pairs with `bg-surface`, `bg-secondary` pairs with `bg-surface-secondary`. Interactive controls take their body colour from `bg-fill`, never from `bg`, so they stay white on either canvas.

## Surface or fill

`bg-surface-*` and `bg-fill-*` often share a colour (`bg-surface-brand` and `bg-fill-brand-subtlest` are both `azure.50`), so never pick between them by colour. Pick by **what the colour belongs to**:

- **Fill:** the colour is the body of the element itself, the shape that *is* the control or the mark. Take the colour away and the element loses its shape.
- **Surface:** the colour is an area that holds other content. Take the colour away and the content is still there, just without a backdrop.

<SurfaceOrFill />

| Use `bg-fill-*` for | Use `bg-surface-*` for |
|---|---|
| Buttons, including the tinted secondary, tertiary and text states | Page sections, wells and grouped areas |
| Checkbox and radio boxes, switch tracks and thumbs | Cards, sheets, dialogs and popovers |
| Chips, segmented control tracks and thumbs | Menu panels and list groups |
| Slider tracks and halos | Banners, snackbars and tooltips |
| Input field bodies | Tappable rows and cards, at rest and on hover or press |
| Badges, counters and status dots | Scrims and dark overlays (`bg-surface-inverse`) |
| Icon wells, such as the leading circle of a list row | A disabled section or card (`bg-surface-disabled`) |
| Skeleton blocks | |

- **Tappable rows stay surfaces.** A menu item or list row is interactive, but it is a slice of its container and holds other content. Its hover and pressed steps come from the surface it sits in: `bg-surface-hover`, `bg-surface-secondary-hover`, or `bg-surface-warning-hover` for a destructive item. If an element holds other components or lines of content, it is a surface; if it is a single control or mark, it is a fill.
- **A control inside a surface is still a fill.** A button in a snackbar or a chip on a card takes `bg-fill-*`, whatever it sits on.
- **Disabled follows the same split.** A disabled control uses `bg-fill-disabled-*`; an unavailable section or card uses `bg-surface-disabled`. The one exception is the disabled chip and the disabled list icon well, which use `bg-surface-disabled-subtle` (`neutral.50`): no disabled fill is that light, and `bg-fill-disabled-subtlest` (`neutral.100`) would vanish on the grey canvas.
- **Pair the foreground with the same role.** On a fill, use `*-on-bg-fill-*` labels and icons; on a surface, use `*-on-bg-surface-*`. Keep the pairing even where the two resolve to the same colour, so they can diverge later, for example in another brand.

## Surface

Containers that hold content: sections, cards, sheets, menus, list groups, banners, tooltips and snackbars, and the tappable rows inside them.

## Fill

The body of an element itself: controls such as buttons, checkboxes, chips, switches and inputs, and marks such as badges, status dots and icon wells. Strong, subtle and subtlest fills each have their own text partner.

<ColorRole role="fill" showPreview={false} />

## Text

Text tokens show their contrast against the surface they are designed for. The `on-bg-fill` and `on-bg-surface` variants are the contrast-safe partners for the matching background.
